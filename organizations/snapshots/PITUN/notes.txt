## 1. Route

Registered route: member roster, enumerated in full from an archived snapshot.
Followed as registered. No gate, no authentication, no obstacle.

Three directory pages at `https://pit-un.org/directory/`, paginated
`?query-7-page=N`, 24 members per page, **72 distinct members**. Each directory
entry gives only a name, a link to a PIT-UN entity page, and a regional hub.

**The entity pages are part of the capture, not an extra.** PIT-UN lists
universities, so Rule 1 applies to every record: it resolves to the unit the
FRAME ITSELF NAMES, and the parent institution does not enter the sample. The
directory names no unit. The entity pages carry Designee, Positions,
Co-Designee, Co-Positions and Website, and those fields are the frame's naming
of a unit — for Arizona State, the positions name the School for the Future of
Innovation in Society and the Julie Ann Wrigley Global Futures Laboratory, and
the website field gives pit.asu.edu alongside asu.edu.

Capturing the directory alone would have sent all 72 records to O3 with nothing
for Rule 1 to resolve against, and Block A would have yielded nothing.

Saved: `directory/page_1..3.html`, `entities/<slug>.html`,
`entity_index.json`, `pitun_members.csv` (the frame of record, named in
`primary.txt`), `fetch_manifest.json`.

**Archived.** All three directory pages were saved to the Wayback Machine on
the capture date — separately, because `?query-7-page=N` are distinct URLs to
the archive and saving page 1 alone would have archived 24 of the 72 members.
URLs in `wayback.txt`. Unlike FORD, the site is ungated and the archive
succeeded, so the snapshots corroborate the capture rather than standing in for
it: anyone can re-fetch pit-un.org and check.

The directory snapshots cover the roster but **not** the fields Rule 1 resolves
against, which live on the 58 entity pages. A small selection of entity pages
is being archived to corroborate the specific claims in §3 and §4 rather than
all 58; the selection and its reasoning are recorded in `wayback.txt`.

## 3. FINDING: PIT-UN names no unit for 19% of its members

Fourteen of 72 members are listed in the directory but their entity page does
not resolve — the server answers with the directory itself. **Verified in a
browser, logged out, on the capture date**, so this is a property of the frame
and not an artifact of scripted access.

For those members the frame names no designee, no position, no unit and no
website. The members:

Boston University; Columbia University; Cornell University; Florida
International University; Lane College; Nazareth University; Northeastern
University; Prairie View A&M University; Princeton University; RAND School of
Public Policy; Stillman College; The Pennsylvania State University; University
of Szeged (Hungary); University of the South (Sewanee).

**No shared property explains the gap.** They are not a recent intake — Columbia,
Cornell, Princeton, Penn State, Northeastern and Boston University are
long-standing members and appear in the directory listing with normal region
tags. The group mixes large private research universities, HBCUs (Lane,
Stillman, Prairie View A&M), an international member (Szeged), and one entity
that is not a university at all (RAND School of Public Policy). The most likely
explanation is an unmaintained content-management gap on PIT-UN's side, but
that is inference and is recorded as such.

**Independently corroborated.** On 2026-09-24 a Wayback Machine save was
requested for `https://pit-un.org/entity/columbia-university/`. The Internet
Archive's crawler was served the member directory and archived that instead:
the resulting snapshot is filed under `pit-un.org/directory/`
(`.../web/20260924064248/https://pit-un.org/directory/`). A third party with no
connection to this project therefore encountered the same substitution, on the
capture date. Recorded with its requested URL in `wayback.txt`; the two must be
read together, since the snapshot alone looks like an ordinary directory save.

**A control rules out the obvious alternative explanation.** Four minutes after
the Columbia save, a Wayback save was requested for
`https://pit-un.org/entity/arizona-state-university/` and archived normally
under its own entity URL
(`.../web/20260924064606/https://pit-un.org/entity/arizona-state-university/`).
Same crawler, same session. The archive reaches entity pages when they exist,
so the Columbia result is not a crawler limitation, a rate limit, or an
artifact of how entity URLs are requested. The two snapshots together are the
strongest evidence for this finding and should be cited as a pair.

**Handling.** The records are retained — dropping them would understate the
frame's membership, which is 72 — with `entity_page_missing = TRUE` and every
entity-page field empty. `listing_text` records why it is empty. They will log
as `unit_unresolved` at O3, be counted, and feed sensitivity check (h) as part
of the bound on what the unit rule and the frame boundaries exclude.

**Why this matters beyond the count.** The register treats PIT-UN as an
identity-type frame: organizations join and thereby declare themselves. The
unit-resolution rule depends on the frame naming a unit. For 19% of members it
names nothing.

This is also a second, independent reason for excluding Block A from the
Block B to Block C unit-resolution comparison. The register's stated reason is
that PIT-UN lists universities, so the block's rate is set by a listing
convention rather than by the tradition's organizational form. This finding
adds that the rate is also set by how completely PIT-UN maintains its own
directory — likewise nothing to do with organizational form. The exclusion was
already registered; this strengthens rather than changes it.

**Consequence for Block A's yield.** Before C1–C5 and before Rule 1
resolution, the realistic ceiling for this frame is the 55 members that name a
unit, not 72. Ford is the block's other frame.

## 4. Two source defects worth knowing about

**The website is often in the anchor text, not the href.** Several entity pages
render the Website field as an anchor whose href is an unsaved WordPress
placeholder (`_wp_link_placeholder`) while the anchor text holds the real URL:

    <a href=".../_wp_link_placeholder">https://direitosp.fgv.br/en/...</a>

A first pass read the href and recorded those members as having no website.
The parser now prefers a URL found in the anchor text and falls back to the
href. Any remaining placeholder or pit-un.org value is kept in its own column
(`broken_links`, `pitun_internal_links`) and `website_url` is left BLANK rather
than wrong.

Blank is deliberate. C1 asks whether the candidate has its own web presence,
and a pit-un.org page is PIT-UN's presence, not the member's. More seriously,
at O5 the lexicon proxy fetches `website_url` and pattern-matches the frozen
lexicon against it; pointed at a pit-un.org page it would compute the proxy
over PIT-UN's vocabulary and file the record in the wrong stratum for a reason
unrelated to how the organization describes itself. A blank is resolved by
hand; a wrong URL is silently believed.

**A single URL is often already unit-specific.** Brown's website field gives
`brown.edu/initiatives/data-science/home`, Case Western's `case.edu/issacs/`.
These are the frame naming a unit through the website field even where
Positions does not, and they are available to Rule 1 at O3 on the same terms as
any other field the frame supplies.

## 5. No location field

The directory and entity pages carry a regional HUB (HLC, West, MSCHE,
International) rather than a location. The hub is an accreditation or
geographic grouping, not an address, and `international_flag` is derived from
it as a signal only.

C2 is therefore determined at O4 from each organization's own materials, as
the criterion requires where a frame's location metadata is absent. Consistent
with the CTFG country-missing treatment and the CFA `location_rule` decision:
the boundary does not pre-empt a criterion assigned to a coder.

## 7. Currency of the roster: assessed, not confirmed

The register asks that the 2026–27 cohort be confirmed present before capture.
**It cannot be confirmed from the source.** PIT-UN's directory does not label
members by cohort or admission year, so there is no field on the page that says
whether the current intake is included.

What is observable, on the capture date:

- **72 members**, against the 64 New America reported in 2023. Growth of eight
  is consistent with continued admissions.
- The directory page's `modified_time` reads **2025-07-22** and the footer
  carries **©2025**.

**Assessment recorded by the lead reviewer:** the roster is taken to be as
current as the source permits, and the stale copyright year and `modified_time`
are attributed to site maintenance rather than to a frozen roster. That is a
judgment, not a verification, and is recorded as such so a reader can weigh it.

The consequence if the judgment is wrong is bounded and is a coverage question,
not a validity one: the frame would under-represent recent PIT-UN admissions.
It would not misdescribe any member that is listed. Since the register places
population-level proportions out of scope for this strand, a roster missing a
recent cohort weakens the claim that the frame reaches the whole membership but
does not affect anything computed from the records themselves.

Two things would settle it cheaply if it later matters: a PIT-UN new-member
announcement naming institutions and checking them against
`pitun_members.csv`, or a comparison against the three archived directory pages
at a later date to see whether the roster moves at all.

## 8. AUTHOR-CONFLICTED FRAME — a disclosure not in the registered statement

**The research team is employed at the University of Virginia and is active in
PIT-UN there.** UVA is a PIT-UN member, so this frame surfaces an organization
in which the authors hold a staff role, and it does so through the frame in
which they participate.

**This is not covered by the registered Conflicts of Interest statement.** That
statement discloses one author's role at Code for Charlottesville and his
participation in the Alliance of Civic Technologists. It does not mention UVA,
PIT-UN, or the team's participation in it. The omission is recorded here on the
date it was identified — at capture, before any record was screened or coded —
and must be added to the Conflicts of Interest field in the next
preregistration update.

**What the registered procedure requires**, applied here:

- The UVA record — whichever unit Rule 1 resolves it to — is flagged
  `author_conflicted` in the shared dataset.
- No conflicted author screens, codes, adjudicates or reviews the coding of
  that record at any stage.
- Both research assistants code it independently, and where their codings
  differ **the disagreement is reported rather than adjudicated**, because the
  person who would ordinarily adjudicate is the conflicted party.
- The recusal is logged and reported with the review.

**PIT-UN itself is also implicated.** As a frame operator it enters the
candidate pool by construction under Rule 2, and the team participates in it.
This parallels the Alliance of Civic Technologists, which the registration
already flags as author-conflicted for the same reason — participation in a
network that is both a frame and a record. Treat the PIT-UN operator record as
author-conflicted on the same terms, and record that reading in the update
rather than leaving it to inference.

**So this frame yields at least two conflicted records**: the UVA member record
and the PIT-UN operator record. Block B yields two more by the same logic —
Code for Charlottesville and ACT.

**The residual limitation the registration already acknowledges applies with
particular force here.** The research assistants are supervised by the
conflicted authors, so their independence is structural rather than complete.
Independent double coding and the reporting of unadjudicated disagreement make
any resulting bias visible in the shared data; they do not eliminate it.
Readers should weigh the coding of these records accordingly.

**Action required before coding begins:** confirm which unit the frame names
for UVA (check `pitun_members.csv` — Positions, Co-Positions and Website), so
the conflicted record is identified in advance rather than recognised
mid-coding.
