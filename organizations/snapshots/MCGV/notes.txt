## 1. Route

Registered route followed. The grants database at
`https://www.mcgovern.org/grants/` offers a **"Download as CSV file"** control,
so the frame was captured as a single export rather than scraped: **890 grant
rows**, matching the site's 89 listing pages at 10 per page.

Columns: `Grantee`, `Fiscal Sponsor`, `Grant`, `Amount`, `Focus Area`,
`Location`, `Date`.

**890 against the register's 795.** The register's figure came from the 990-PF
grantee database. The live database covers July 2017 to now and is updated
weekly, so it runs ahead of the tax filings. Not a discrepancy — different
sources, and the live database is the one the register names.

**Captured unfiltered.** The register names no focus-area filter for this
frame, unlike FORD. Focus areas include McGovern's legacy brain-research
giving — Neuroscience, Legacy, DAF, Matching Gift, N/A — which C3 removes by
asking whether the core activity survives without the technology. That judgment
belongs to the criterion, not to a capture-time filter.

## 2. The CSV has no URL column, and the gap was closed separately

The export gives no website for any grantee. The rendered listing pages do
carry a link on every grantee card, so those 89 pages were fetched and the URLs
joined back on by `mcgv_urls.py` (kept in this directory), producing
`mcgv_grantee_urls.csv` with `Grantee`, `website_url` and `url_source`.

This was not optional. `website_url` does two jobs downstream and a blank
breaks both: it is where C1 and C2 start at O4, on the largest frame in the
register, and at O5 the lexicon proxy FETCHES it and pattern-matches the frozen
lexicon against the page. With nothing to fetch, the proxy returns undetermined
and every record has to be resolved by hand before the coding queue can be
built.

**Result.** 450 distinct grantees; **334 have a website in the frame** (267 as
absolute URLs, 67 as bare domains), **116 do not** — their card carries a
`grantee-website` anchor with no href at all. Among those without: University
of Southampton, Jhpiego Corporation, Bird Story Agency, Brain Builders Youth
Development Initiative, Native BioData Consortium, ReFED, Open Climate Fix,
OceanMind.

**The join is exact: all 450 CSV grantee names were found in the listing
pages, with zero mismatches.** The export and the rendered listing are
evidently generated from the same source rather than maintained separately,
which is worth knowing — it means the CSV can be trusted as a complete
rendering of what the pages show.

### A defect in the frame, recorded because it misled an earlier pass

McGovern writes many grantee links as a **bare domain with no scheme**:

    <a class="grantee-website" href="cworthy.org"></a>

A scheme-less href resolves relative to the current page, so a browser turns
that into `mcgovern.org/grants/cworthy.org` — a dead link. **Those grantee
links are broken on McGovern's own site.** The domain in the attribute is
correct; only the missing scheme is wrong, so the site's defect costs this
study nothing once read from the source and given a scheme. 67 of 334
recovered URLs came this way.

This is recorded because it produced a false explanation before it produced a
correct one. An early fetch of the rendered page showed URLs of the form
`mcgovern.org/grants/cworthy.org`, and these notes briefly described McGovern
as "encoding the grantee's domain in its own URLs" — a site convention that
does not exist. Those URLs were a **browser artifact of relative resolution**,
not something in the markup. The first version of `mcgv_urls.py` was written
against that misreading and, combined with a wrong assumption about where the
anchor sits in the card, recovered 6 names across 89 pages instead of 450.

The parser now reads the card's own classes — `article.grants-tease`,
`h2.grantee-name`, `a.grantee-website` — rather than inferring structure from a
rendered view.

**What is never recorded as a grantee's website is a mcgovern.org URL.** It is
the funder's own page, and at O5 the lexicon proxy would score the FUNDER's
vocabulary instead of the grantee's, filing the record in the wrong stratum for
a reason having nothing to do with how the organization describes itself — the
same mistake that put pit-un.org into PIT-UN's `website_url`.

**The frame of record is the MERGED file, not the download.** `mcgv_urls.py
join` writes `mcgv_grants_with_urls.csv` — the 890 grant rows with
`website_url` and `url_source` appended — and writes `primary.txt` naming it,
in the same step. The enumerator reads one file per frame and needs
`website_url` on the same rows as the grants; naming the download in
`primary.txt` would hash a file missing the column that O4 and O5 depend on.
Writing both together is what stops them drifting apart. This directory holds
several CSVs and two manifests, so `capture_log.py` would otherwise refuse to
guess.

**`url_source` records how each URL was obtained**, because the three cases are
not equally strong evidence: an absolute URL is what the frame published, a
bare domain is what the frame meant to publish, and a blank is what the frame
does not have. The 116 blanks are left blank rather than guessed; C1 and C2 are
determined at O4 from the organization's own materials.

## 3. What to expect at screening

**Heavy repetition — the unit is grants, not organizations.** 890 grants
resolve to **450 distinct grantee names**, about two grants each — lower than
the register's estimate of 3-7 relationships per organization, which was drawn
from the 990-PF data over a different span. Note this is a count of distinct
NAMES; deduplication at O3 may reduce it further where one organization is
named inconsistently across grants. MIT, Trek Medics,
CarbonPlan, Blue Star Families, Materiom, Upsolve, Jhpiego, Poynter, Givedignifiedwork
(Karya) and The Conversation each appear several times in the first two years
alone; MIT at least four times. The reader collapses these to organizations
with a `grant_count`, so 890 grants will yield far fewer organizations. The
register's estimate of 3–7 relationships per organization looks right.

**Heavy fiscal sponsorship.** Convergent Research, FFWD (Fast Forward), GDI
Solutions, Social Good Fund, New Venture Fund, Allied Media Projects, Community
Initiatives, Mantra4Change US, Africa No Filter Foundation, Edward Charles
Foundation, Global Impact, Sand County Foundation, Res Non Verba, National
Center for Civic Innovation and Social Science Research Council all appear in
the Fiscal Sponsor column. Every one fires the Rule 1 fiscal-sponsor clause at
O3: resolve to the sponsored project if the frame names it and it meets C1
independently, otherwise to the sponsor, recording the routing — never both.

**Heavy C2 attrition.** The most international frame in the register by a wide
margin: GBR, KEN, IND, BRA, CHE, ARG, COL, DEU, FRA, ZAF, NGA, ETH, PAK, AUT,
CHL, NCL and more. The foundation's own 2025 announcement described 149 grants
across 13 countries.

**Institutions appear, so Rule 1 applies case by case.** MIT, Northeastern,
Brown, The George Washington University, UCSF Foundation, Lahore University of
Management Sciences, University of Southampton. `lists_institutions` is false
for this frame — most grantees are organizations — so records do not arrive
PENDING in bulk; the institutional ones are resolved individually at O3.

## 4. Location is the grantee's, not the beneficiary's

Unlike Fast Forward, whose Regions field records where the work lands, this
frame's `Location` is the grantee's own location as the funder records it:
"Boulder, CO, USA", "Nairobi, 110, KEN", "London, LND, GBR". Mapped, and much
closer to C2 than FF's field.

Still not C2. It is the funder's record of the grantee, and C2 is a human
determination at O4 from the organization's own materials. No location filter
was applied at capture. Consistent with CTFG's missing country field, the CFA
location rule, PIT-UN's regional hubs and FF's regions.

## 5. Observed Block C overlap

**FFWD (Fast Forward) appears here twice as a grantee** — 2024 and 2025,
$500,000 each — **and as a fiscal sponsor** for Materiom C.I.C. and Climate
Cabinet Education. The Block C overlap anticipated in `../FF/notes.txt` §6 is
therefore observed rather than predicted.

Within block, so these records do not enter the multi-frame subset used in
sensitivity check (f), which requires frames from two or more blocks.
Multi-frame provenance is retained in the data. Frame redundancy within Block C
is reported as description and is not compared across blocks as evidence of
relative population size — the register names this case directly, since a
funder that supports the accelerator whose portfolio another frame enumerates
manufactures overlap independently of field size.

One record worth noting in advance: the **Public Interest Technology (PIT)
Infrastructure Fund** appears as a 2024 grantee, fiscally sponsored by New
Venture Fund. A PIT-named entity surfacing through a Block C funder portfolio
is exactly the kind of cross-tradition blur RQ5 exists to find. It is recorded
here as an observation, not as a finding; it will be screened and coded like
any other record.

## 6. Archiving, and a limitation stated plainly

Four of 89 pages are archived (see `wayback.txt`): page 1 for size, page 2 for
recency, page 49 for the midpoint, page 89 for depth back to 2017.

A Wayback save of the **CSV endpoint was attempted first and failed** — the
download control carries an empty href, so the file is assembled client-side
with no stable URL for the archive to fetch. That attempt is recorded because
it explains why the weaker route was used.

**This frame is updated WEEKLY**, the fastest-moving in the register. A
re-fetch a month from now returns a materially different set. The archive is
therefore doing more work here than anywhere else, and four pages is thin: a
reader can verify the frame's shape and span from a third party, but not its
contents. The downloaded CSV, hashed in `capture_log.json` and deposited on
OSF, is the record of this frame.

## 7. Rule 2: the McGovern Foundation itself

PJMF operates this frame and enters the candidate pool by construction as a
Rule 2 operator, screened on C1–C5 and coded on the full instrument like any
other record, flagged as an operator, and excluded from every within-block rate
and from the saturation count because its inclusion is guaranteed rather than
sampled.

No author conflict is known for this frame.
