## 1. Route: four cohorts, three sources, one pending

Google.org maintains no standing portfolio, so the register defines this frame
as the union of four cohorts, named explicitly. Cohorts announced after the
freeze date do not enter the frame regardless of when they are announced.

| Cohort | Source | Records |
|---|---|---|
| 2019 AI Impact Challenge | `impactchallenge.withgoogle.com/ai2018` | 20 |
| 2025 Google.org Accelerator: Generative AI | `impactchallenge.withgoogle.com/genaiaccelerator` | 20 |
| 2026 Impact Challenge: AI for Government Innovation | `google.org/impact-challenges/ai-government-innovation/` | 15 |
| 2026 Impact Challenge: AI for Science | `google.org/impact-challenges/ai-science/` | **0 — pending** |

**55 organizations** across the three published cohorts. Each cohort's size was
known in advance from Google's own announcements, and `parse` refuses to write
anything unless every cohort matches its expected count (§4).

**Naming note.** Google's URL and page title for the first cohort say *2018*;
the register calls it the *2019 AI Impact Challenge*. Same cohort — launched
2018, grantees announced May 2019. Recorded so the discrepancy is not later
mistaken for two different programmes.

## 2. AI for Science: pending, and the trap on its page

Applications closed 2026-05-01 and **no recipient list had been published** as
of the capture date. Under the register's provision, the cohort is recorded as
pending: enumerate it if and when its list publishes, recording *that* date
separately from the freeze date, and report it as unenumerated if it never
publishes.

The page is captured as **dated evidence of non-publication**, and the Wayback
snapshot (`wayback.txt`) is third-party corroboration of the same fact — the
Internet Archive found no recipient list on 2026-09-24 either. Without that,
the claim rests on this project's own fetch log.

**The trap.** That page carries six organizations under *Previously funded
recipients*. They belong to the **earlier inaugural AI for Science fund**, not
to the named 2026 cohort. `parse` extracts nothing from this page by design, so
they cannot leak in; the enumerator's cohort guard would reject them as a
second line of defence. Recorded because the page reads as though those six are
the cohort's recipients, and nothing on it says otherwise.

## 3. The 2024 Generative AI cohort is not in the frame

The Generative AI Accelerator ran twice: **21 organizations in 2024, 20 in
2025**. The register names only the 2025 cohort.

The cohort page lists the 2025 participants and mentions 2024 only in prose, so
capturing the page is correct. The risk is elsewhere: a blog post about "the
Generative AI Accelerator" may list the 2024 cohort, and those must not be
added by hand.

## 4. Why every cohort carries a declared size

Each cohort's count is declared in `gorg_fetch.py`, and `parse` refuses the
whole frame if any cohort misses. This is not belt-and-braces; it is the only
reason four separate parsing failures in this project were caught rather than
shipped.

For this frame the guard caught two over-counts on the first run — 21 against
20, and 23 against 15 — which resolved to `g.co`, six Google properties under
the `.google` top-level domain, and two partner links carrying no name
(apolitical.co, centreforpublicimpact.org). A link with no accompanying text is
a partner logo, not a recipient.

## 5. A parsing lesson recorded, because it recurred

The first parse of the 2019 cohort returned 20 records with names like
*"American University of Beirut Millions of people in the Middle East…"* and
*"Rainforest"* instead of *Rainforest Connection*. The count was right; the
data was not.

Cause: the parser looked for `h2`–`h5` headings, found none, and fell back to
splitting a flattened string on a list of opening verbs. The page in fact
renders each grantee in Google's "glue" card components, which state every
field outright:

    <p class="glue-label">UNITED KINGDOM</p>
    <p class="glue-headline">Full Fact</p>
    <p class="glue-body">As concerns about mis- and disinformation grow…</p>

**This was the fourth failure of the same kind in this strand** — after FORD's
pagination, PIT-UN's entity anchors and McGovern's grantee cards. Every one had
the same cause: structure inferred from rendered text instead of read from the
page's own elements, and every fix was the same shape. The page always said
what it meant.

**Why truncated names would have mattered more than they look.** Deduplication
at O3 matches on name. Penn State and Columbia both appear in PIT-UN, and those
are cross-block multi-frame records — among the few that enter sensitivity
check (f), which requires frames from two or more blocks. A name with a
description glued onto it would never have matched, the subset would have come
up short, and nothing would have errored.

## 6. Country and region: what they are, and what they are not

The 2019 cohort states each grantee's **country** outright in `glue-label` —
LEBANON, UNITED STATES, UGANDA, COLOMBIA. That is the best C2 material in this
frame and is captured to its own column. It is matched against a country list
rather than a leading-capitals rule, because organization names are capitalised
too: TOJIL and CIV:LAB would otherwise read as countries.

The 2025 cohort instead gives a **regional grouping** — Americas, Asia Pacific,
Europe/Middle East & Africa, Global — which is mixed in meaning: Technical
University of Munich's EMEA matches its headquarters, while Red de Innovación
Local sits under Americas and works across Latin America.

**Neither is C2.** C2 asks about headquarters or principal operations and is
determined at O4 from each organization's own materials. `listing_text` labels
the region field "as the cohort page reports it, NOT headquarters" so a coder
reading a record at O3 cannot mistake it for C2 evidence. No location filter was
applied at capture, consistent with CTFG, CFA, PIT-UN, FF and MCGV.

**Expect the heaviest C2 attrition in the register.** These are global open
calls: the 2019 cohort alone spans Lebanon, Uganda, Colombia, Indonesia, India,
Switzerland, Brazil, France, Australia and the Netherlands, and the 2026
government cohort adds Japan, Germany, Nigeria, Italy and the United Kingdom.

## 7. Institutions, and a cross-block record to watch

Several recipients are universities rather than organizations: American
University of Beirut, Makerere, Colegio Mayor de Nuestra Señora del Rosario,
Penn State, Columbia, Johns Hopkins, MIT, Warwick, Cincinnati, Nagoya, Aga Khan,
Technical University of Munich. `lists_institutions` is false for this frame —
most recipients are organizations — so records do not arrive PENDING in bulk,
and Rule 1 is applied individually at O3 where a recipient is an institution.

Note that several links already point at the funded unit rather than the
parent: `cee.psu.edu`, `transitlab.mit.edu`, `cs.mak.ac.ug`, `saferoadsai.org`.
Where the frame names a unit, Rule 1 resolves to that unit.

**The Pennsylvania State University and Columbia University both appear in
PIT-UN** — and both are among PIT-UN's 14 members with no entity page (see
`../PITUN/notes.txt` §3). They are therefore Block A / Block C multi-frame
records and enter the multi-frame subset of sensitivity check (f), which is
otherwise expected to be small. Worth flagging in advance so their
deduplication is verified rather than assumed.

## 8. Rule 2: Google.org itself

Google.org operates this frame and enters the candidate pool by construction as
a Rule 2 operator, screened on C1–C5 and coded on the full instrument like any
other record, flagged as an operator, and excluded from every within-block rate
and from the saturation count because its inclusion is guaranteed rather than
sampled.

No author conflict is known for this frame.

## 9. Observed Block C overlap

Third confirmation of the overlap anticipated in `../FF/notes.txt` §6:
**Lenny Learning, One Degree, Visilant and Darsel** are Fast Forward alumni
appearing in the 2025 Generative AI cohort, and **Upsolve** and **SkillUp

reported as description and not compared across blocks as evidence of relative
population size.
