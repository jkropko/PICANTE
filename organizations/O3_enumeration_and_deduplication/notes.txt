## 1. Route, and a boundary that lives in the URL

Registered route followed. **102 organizations**, matching Fast Forward's own
reported total exactly, captured from 11 pages of:

    https://www.ffwd.org/directory?portfolio=true

`https://www.ffwd.org/alumni/` redirects there. Every record carries an "FFWD
Portfolio" badge, a link to the organization's own site, a description, issue
areas, regions and technologies.

**The live listing will move.** The 2027 Accelerator was taking applications on
the capture date, so new cohorts will join the portfolio. ffwd.org is ungated,
so anyone can re-fetch it — but a later re-fetch will not reproduce the frozen
frame, only the frame as it stands then. The archived pages are the only route
back to the freeze state, and at present one of eleven is archived
(`wayback.txt`).

**`portfolio=true` is the frame definition, not a convenience.** Fast Forward
publishes two listings from the same directory:

    /directory                    1,759 results
    /directory?portfolio=true       102 results

The register's frame is the accelerator portfolio. The full directory is "every
tech nonprofit that we know about", and organizations enter it **by submitting
a form**. That is a self-declaration admission mechanism, where this frame's
registered admission mechanism is **selection**. Capturing the directory would
not merely have inflated the frame seventeenfold; it would have changed what
Block C is sampling and corrupted `admission_mechanism`, a variable the
analysis records specifically because it is a confound it must hold rather than
ignore.

The boundary is therefore applied at capture, by the URL, rather than in
`frames.json`. This is the only frame in the register where that is true, and
it is recorded in `ffwd_capture_manifest.json` so the reason travels with the
data. The fetch script refuses to run if the reported count exceeds 500, which
is what a failure of the filter would look like.

## 2. The Geographies field is NOT C2, and no location filter was applied

Each record carries "Regions". These are the geographies the organization's
**work serves**, not where it is based:

    Bayes Impact            France
    Adalat AI               Ghana, India
    Black Sisters in STEM   Africa, Belgium, Canada, Germany, U.S.
    AccesSOS                U.S.

Black Sisters in STEM is US-based with international reach; Adalat AI works in
Indian courts. Filtering the directory's Geographies control to "U.S." would
**drop US organizations working abroad and admit foreign organizations working
in the US** — wrong in both directions.

No location filter was applied at capture. C2 asks about headquarters or
principal operations and is a human determination at O4 from each
organization's own materials. Same position as CTFG's missing country field,
the CFA location rule, and PIT-UN's regional hubs: the frame boundary does not
pre-empt a criterion the registration assigns to a coder.

**55 of 102 list "U.S." as their only region.** That number is recorded as a
property of the frame and **is not a C2 estimate or a prediction of one.**
`listing_text` labels the field "(BENEFICIARY, not HQ)" so a coder reading a
record at O3 cannot mistake it for C2 evidence.

Expect substantial C2 attrition regardless. The 2026 cohort alone spans the
United States, India, France and Mexico.

## 3. A silent exclusion, found and fixed

The first parse returned **101 of 102**. Nothing errored: page 6 held nine
records where every other full page held ten, and the shortfall read like an
off-by-one in how the site counts. It surfaced only because the script compares
captured records against the site's own reported total and says so.

Cause: the parser skipped site chrome and social links by testing whether a
skip string appeared **anywhere in the URL**, and `x.com` was on that list.
**muevete + x.com + .mx** — the Mexican member Muevetex was removed from the
frame because its domain ends in "x" before ".com".

Fixed in v1.0.0 by comparing HOSTS, exactly or as a parent domain, so a skip
entry can never match the middle of an unrelated organization's domain. Ten
test cases cover it, including `relx.com` as a control.

Recorded because the failure mode matters more than the record. A rule that
deletes members by accident of domain spelling is invisible: no error, no gap
in the page, no trace in the output. And it was not deleting at random — it
removed a Mexican organization from a frame where C2 attrition is the central
question, so the loss would have been correlated with exactly the variable this
frame is most sensitive on.

## 4. What the frame gives, and what it does not

**Gives:** organization name, its own website URL, a one- or two-sentence
description, issue areas, regions, technologies. All 102 records carry a
description — the richest `listing_text` of any frame captured so far, and
unlike ACT's bare name-and-URL it will give the O4 coder something to work
with before they open the site.

**Does not give:** headquarters, founding year, legal form, staff, or any
contact detail. Every descriptive field on the instrument comes from the
organization's own materials at O4.

**Not institutions.** Unlike PIT-UN and FORD, this frame lists organizations
directly, so `lists_institutions` is false and records do not arrive PENDING
for Rule 1. Rule 1 still applies where a record turns out to be a fiscally
sponsored project or a program inside a larger body; that is decided
case by case at O3, not flagged in advance.

## 5. Rule 2: Fast Forward itself

Fast Forward operates this frame and therefore enters the candidate pool by
construction as a Rule 2 operator, screened on C1–C5 and coded on the full
instrument like any other record, flagged as an operator, and **excluded from
every within-block rate and from the saturation count** because its inclusion
is guaranteed rather than sampled.

No author conflict is known for this frame. Unlike ACT and PIT-UN, no member of
the team holds a role at Fast Forward or at any portfolio organization so far
as is known; if that changes, the Conflicts of Interest procedure applies.

## 6. Expected overlap within Block C

Fast Forward's accelerator is funded in part by Google.org, and the Patrick J.
McGovern Foundation is among its funders. Some portfolio organizations are
therefore plausibly also McGovern grantees or Google.org cohort members.

All three are Block C, so these records do not enter the multi-frame subset
used in sensitivity check (f), which requires frames from two or more blocks.
Multi-frame provenance is retained in the data.

Frame redundancy within the block is reported as description and **is not
compared across blocks** as evidence of relative population size — the register
names this case directly, since a funder that supports the accelerator whose
portfolio the other frame enumerates manufactures overlap independently of
field size.
